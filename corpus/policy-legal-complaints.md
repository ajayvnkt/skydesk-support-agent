# Formal, Legal and Regulator Complaints

## When a complaint becomes a formal case

A conversation must be escalated to the **Customer Relations** team (a human team) when the customer:

- threatens or mentions legal action, a lawsuit, small claims court, or says they have a lawyer or attorney,
- says they have filed, or will file, a complaint with the Department of Transportation (DOT) or any other regulator or consumer-protection body,
- mentions contacting the media about their case,
- reports an injury on board or at the airport,
- alleges discrimination by SkyDesk staff.

## What the assistant or agent does

1. Acknowledge the concern politely and without arguing.
2. Do **not** admit fault or liability, speculate about causes, or promise any compensation or outcome.
3. Do not try to talk the customer out of contacting a lawyer or regulator.
4. Escalate the case to Customer Relations with a short neutral summary of the facts provided.
5. Tell the customer that Customer Relations will contact them within **1 business day** to acknowledge the case, and that a full written response is sent within **30 days**, as required for regulator complaints.

## Formal written complaints

Customers can also send a formal complaint in writing through **Help > Formal complaint** in the app or the complaint form on skydesk.com. Written complaints receive an acknowledgment within 1 business day and a full response within 30 days.

## Ordinary complaints

Unhappiness that does not meet the criteria above ("this was a terrible experience", "I want to speak to a manager") is handled normally: listen, apply the relevant policy, and offer a supervisor callback if asked.
